What the calculator is solving

Market spacing, dynamic sizing, and the deadline pace guardrail

Market layer. The updater searches rolling lookback × first offset × spacing on BTCUSDT one-minute paths. Every historical reference is the close of the 09:35 ET bar; eligible fills begin at 09:36. Candidate ladders are compared by walk-forward weekly implementation shortfall. A one-standard-error plateau and the prior published parameter regularize the result.

Sizing layer. Once spacing is fixed, shares are a control output: remaining target shares divided by remaining sessions and expected rung hits. Orders continue only until their combined shares equal the effective target, so a deep intraday move cannot over-deploy the account.

Deadline layer. Supplying total capital remaining activates an explicit schedule guardrail. The even-pace floor is total remaining × eligible sessions in the current window ÷ sessions to the deadline; the current window ends at the earlier of week-end or the deadline. The effective target is the larger of that floor and the entered weekly target, capped at total remaining. If the floor causes a pace lift, market distances tighten by 1 ÷ √(pace lift), bounded between 0.40× and 1.00×. This rule is deliberately monotone: moving the deadline closer can never reduce the target or widen the ladder.

The published JSON is still the daily-OHLC seed. Deadline pacing remains available, but market spacing is using fallback parameters until the first successful minute-data update.
