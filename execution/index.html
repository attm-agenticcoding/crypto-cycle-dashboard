    What the calculator is solving

    Market spacing, dynamic sizing, and the deadline pace guardrail

    Market layer. The updater searches rolling lookback × first offset × spacing on the selected instrument's minute data, —. Every historical reference is the close of the 09:35 ET bar; eligible fills begin at 09:36. Candidate ladders are compared by walk-forward weekly implementation shortfall. A paired excess-cost selection band and the prior published parameter regularize the result. The adopted values are a heuristic, not statistically optimal parameters; the eligible band is not a confidence interval.

    24/7 spot pairs. The daily reference is the close of the 00:00 UTC minute, with eligible fills from 00:01 through 23:59. Weekends and holidays count; weeks run Monday–Sunday in UTC. Both directions are fitted separately from the pair's full-day highs and lows. Scaling runs daily at 04:30 UTC with an 08:30 retry after archives become available. GTC orders require manual cancellation or replacement at the next UTC reset. Exchange tick, quantity and notional filters apply; changing market prices can also affect the exchange's dynamic price bands.

    Sizing layer. Once spacing is fixed, quantity is a control output: remaining target quantity divided by remaining sessions and expected rung hits. Buy targets use quote currency; sell targets use shares or the crypto base asset and are capped at holdings after accounting for existing sell orders. Buy prices round down and passive sell prices round up: to cents for listed securities, or the exchange price tick for crypto. Crypto quantities follow the exchange step and minimum order size.

    Deadline layer. Supplying both total remaining and a deadline activates an explicit schedule guardrail. With no deadline, the entered weekly pace is used, capped by total remaining if supplied; market distances are not tightened. The even-pace floor is total remaining × eligible sessions in the current window ÷ sessions to the deadline; the current window ends at the earlier of week-end or the deadline. The effective target is the larger of that floor and the entered weekly target, capped at total remaining. If the floor causes a pace lift, market distances tighten by 1 ÷ √(pace lift), bounded between 0.40× and 1.00×. This rule is deliberately monotone: moving the deadline closer can never reduce the target or widen the ladder.

    Sell scaling. Sell fits use the largest upward move after the reference, independently of buy drawdowns. The objective minimizes sale shortfall: reference value minus sale proceeds, with unfilled weekly shares valued at the next reference. That terminal valuation is a backtest assumption. Expected hits are proxy price touches, not guaranteed fills; the proxy does not capture the listed instrument's bid–ask spread, queue, partial fills or fees.

    Sale priority. Price seeking keeps a passive ladder. Finish by deadline adds a manual closeout 15 minutes before the final eligible session closes (23:45 UTC for crypto): recalculate with updated fills, holdings, outstanding orders and a fresh best bid. It generates a sell limit at that bid, rounded down to the applicable price tick. Check the broker or exchange preview before placing it. A moving quote can leave it unfilled. The deadline and closeout rules are explicit overlays, not separately fitted optima. Nothing on this page submits or cancels orders. See the NYSE session calendar.
